A post is a short text, up to four images, or both, published to the accounts that [follow](/http-api/users/follows/) its author. A post can instead hold one [video](#video-posts), which becomes a post only after it is encoded and checked. A post with text can carry a [poll](#poll-object) instead of media, and a [public](#public-posts) post can also reach other accounts through [Discover](#get-discover-feed). New posts are not pushed over the Gateway; a client reads the [home feed](#get-home-feed). Accounts that may read a post can [like](#like-post) it, [comment](#create-comment) on it and [reply](#create-reply) to its comments. Likes, comments, replies and new followers reach the account they are aimed at as [notifications](#list-notifications). Posts and comments are reported through [Report feed post](/http-api/reports/#report-feed-post) and [Report feed comment](/http-api/reports/#report-feed-comment).

Every route here except [Receive Bunny Stream webhook](#receive-bunny-stream-webhook) requires a user session. A bot or OAuth2 bearer credential is refused with 403 `ACCESS_DENIED`, and a [limited account](/http-api/users/#account-limitation) is refused with 403 `ACCOUNT_LIMITED` by every route that writes. While the `feature_feed` limit of the caller is `0`, every route that requires a session returns 403 `FEATURE_TEMPORARILY_DISABLED`.

:::note[Posts use the profile rule]
Reading an account's posts needs the full profile under [Get user profile](/http-api/users/#get-user-profile). Any other reader receives 403 `MISSING_ACCESS`, and so does a block in either direction. The caller can always read its own posts. A [public post](#public-posts) relaxes the rule for that post, and [List user posts](#list-user-posts) returns the public posts to a caller that sees the public profile.
:::

## Public posts

A post is marked public when its author's [profile privacy level](/http-api/users/#profile-privacy-levels) reads as `EVERYONE` at the time it is created; the server sets the mark and ignores the `public` field of the request. Posts marked earlier keep the mark, including those an earlier client marked while the author was at `ALL_GUILDS`. While the author's level reads as `EVERYONE`, any account with a user session can read a public post by its ID, like it, comment on it and vote in its poll without the profile rule, unless either account blocks the other or the author is disabled or deleted. Once the level reads as anything else, the post falls back to the profile rule on the next request. The public post of an account flagged as a spammer keeps the profile rule. [List user posts](#list-user-posts) and [List post likers](#list-post-likers) keep the profile rule for every post. On a public post of another account, [List comments](#list-comments) leaves out the comments of accounts that block the caller or that the caller blocks. An account that reaches a public post only through its public flag needs more to write there: see [Create comment](#create-comment) and [Vote in poll](#vote-in-poll).

## Video posts

A video post is uploaded to Bunny Stream rather than through [Upload post media](#upload-post-media). It needs a `feature_feed_video` limit other than `0` for the caller and the instance's Bunny Stream settings; without either, [Start video post](#start-video-post) and [Re-sign video upload](#re-sign-video-upload) return 403 `FEATURE_TEMPORARILY_DISABLED`.

1. [Start video post](#start-video-post) returns a [video upload ticket](#video-upload-ticket-object). It takes one post of the day at once, and an account has at most 3 videos on their way.
2. Upload the file with TUS to `upload.endpoint`, sending `upload.signature` as `AuthorizationSignature`, `upload.expires` as `AuthorizationExpire`, `id` as `VideoId` and `library_id` as `LibraryId`. The signature lasts 2 hours; [Re-sign video upload](#re-sign-video-upload) signs the same upload again within 6 hours of its start.
3. Bunny Stream encodes the video and reports each step to [Receive Bunny Stream webhook](#receive-bunny-stream-webhook). Once it is encoded, the video is checked for explicit content through Bunny's content tagging and frames of the video.
4. A clean video becomes a post with the text given at the start, public when the author's level read as `EVERYONE` at the start, and reaches home feeds and Discover like any post. No route shows the video before that.

A video that fails to encode, is stored larger than 500 MB, is refused by the check or is still not a post after 72 hours is deleted, and its author receives a system DM unless the account is disabled or deleted. [List pending videos](#list-pending-videos) shows the caller's videos on their way. A published video that Bunny later tags as adult, or whose upload is replaced, loses its post.

## Video object

The URLs are signed for the request that returned them and name only this video's files. They stay the same within each hour and work until `expires_at`, at least 2 hours after the request (longer for a video longer than 90 minutes, at most about 7 hours); read the post again for new ones.

### Structure

| Field | Type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| id | string | The Bunny Stream ID (GUID) of the video |
| width | ?integer | The width of the picture as shown, in pixels |
| height | ?integer | The height of the picture as shown, in pixels |
| duration | ?integer | The length in seconds |
| poster_url | string | The signed URL of the poster image |
| hls_url | string | The signed URL of the HLS playlist |
| mp4_url<sup>1</sup> | ?string | The signed URL of an MP4 file for players without HLS |
| expires_at | ISO8601 timestamp | When the signed URLs stop working |

<sup>1</sup> The 720p file, or the largest one below it; `null` when Bunny keeps no MP4 of the video

## Notification object

The actions of one type aimed at one account fold into one notification per post and clock hour (per clock hour for follows). A later action of the same hour updates the notification in place under the same `id`.

### Structure

| Field | Type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| id<sup>1</sup> | snowflake | The notification ID |
| type<sup>5</sup> | string | `post_like`, `post_comment`, `comment_reply` or `follow` |
| post_id | ?snowflake | The post liked or commented on; `null` for `follow` |
| comment_id<sup>2</sup> | ?snowflake | The newest comment for `post_comment`, the comment the newest reply answers for `comment_reply`, else `null` |
| actors<sup>3</sup> | array[[partial user](/http-api/users/#partial-user-object) object] | Up to three of the accounts behind it, the most recent first |
| actor_count<sup>4</sup> | integer | How many accounts are behind it |
| updated_at | ISO8601 timestamp | When the newest action folded into it happened |

<sup>1</sup> Its [snowflake](/snowflakes/) timestamp is when its first action was folded in. It is the `before` cursor of [List notifications](#list-notifications)

<sup>2</sup> The comment may have been deleted since; the post then still lists its other comments

<sup>3</sup> Holds at least one account. Accounts the recipient blocks are left out, and a notification with no other account is left out entirely

<sup>4</sup> Counts each account once while it stays among the three newest; an account that undoes and repeats a like after three others may count again. Undoing a like or deleting a comment does not lower it

<sup>5</sup> `comment_reply` goes to the author of a comment that receives a reply, and only while the instance's `feature_feed_reply_notifications` limit is not `0`. A client ignores a type it does not know

## Notification state object

The caller's notification read cursor and unread count, sent in [READY](/gateway/events/#ready-object) as `feed_notification_state`. It is the same on all of the caller's devices.

### Structure

| Field | Type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| last_read_at | ?ISO8601 timestamp | The read cursor that [Mark notifications read](#mark-notifications-read) moves; `null` before the first read |
| unread_count<sup>1</sup> | integer | Notifications updated after `last_read_at` (0-10) |
| unread_seq<sup>2</sup> | integer | The unread sequence this count includes |
| unread_epoch<sup>3</sup> | integer | When that sequence began, in Unix milliseconds; `0` before it exists |

<sup>1</sup> Counted over the newest 20 notifications of the newest 20 days and capped at 10, so a client shows `9+` from 10. A notification whose post was deleted counts until the notifications are read

<sup>2</sup> [FEED_NOTIFICATION_CREATE](/gateway/events/#feed-notification-create) carries newer values; the rise over the last value a client saw is the number of newly unread notifications

<sup>3</sup> The sequence lives for a day after its last change (a newly unread notification, a read or a recount) and then starts again from 0 under a later epoch. A value under a later epoch than a client's counts from 0; one under an earlier epoch is a late event and changes no count

## Upload post media

<RouteHeader method="POST" path="/v1/feed/attachments" />

Plans the upload of up to four post images and returns 200 with `attachments`, an array of [singlepart attachment upload objects](/http-api/messages/#singlepart-attachment-upload-object) in request order. Each object echoes the `id` of its request item. Upload each file with one `PUT` to its `upload_url`, then pass the `upload_filename` in [Create post](#create-post). A video is not uploaded here; it starts at [Start video post](#start-video-post).

A filename must name a PNG, JPEG, WebP or GIF image, and a declared size may not exceed 10 MiB or the caller's `max_attachment_file_size` limit when that is lower. The instance must allow presigned uploads.

### JSON body

| Field | Type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| attachments | array[[attachment upload request item](/http-api/messages/#attachment-upload-request-item-object) object] | The images to upload (1-4) |

### Response

| Status | Body | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 200 | object with `attachments` | The upload URLs were issued |
| 400<sup>1</sup> | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | The body is invalid, a filename is not an image, or a size is too large |
| 403<sup>2</sup> | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | Presigned uploads are off and the request returns `FEATURE_TEMPORARILY_DISABLED` |

<sup>1</sup> A filename that is not a PNG, JPEG, WebP or GIF returns `INVALID_FORM_BODY` with the field code `ATTACHMENT_MUST_BE_IMAGE`; a size over the limit returns `FILE_SIZE_TOO_LARGE`

<sup>2</sup> Also returned for an MP4 or WebM filename while the caller's `feature_feed_video` limit is `0`, as before video posts moved to Bunny Stream; once it is not `0`, such a filename returns 400 with `ATTACHMENT_MUST_BE_IMAGE`

### Rate limit

10 requests per 10 seconds for each authenticated user, on the `feed:attachment:upload` bucket.

## Create post

<RouteHeader method="POST" path="/v1/feed/posts" />

Publishes a post and returns 200 with the [post object](#post-object). A post needs text, media, or both. A poll needs text, which is its question, and takes no media.

Each image is checked like a message attachment, and more strictly: the media type sniffed from the uploaded bytes must be PNG, JPEG, WebP or GIF, the file may not exceed 10 MiB, explicit media is refused, and a file the media service cannot read is refused rather than kept as a plain file. An upload key is consumed by the post that uses it. A video post is made through [Start video post](#start-video-post) instead.

The post is [public](#public-posts), and can reach [Discover](#get-discover-feed), when the caller's profile privacy level reads as `EVERYONE`; `public` in the body is ignored. The first link of the text that can be previewed becomes the post's [link preview](#link-preview-object).

An account can publish `max_feed_posts_per_day` posts a feed day (50 by default). Followers see the first `feed_fanout_max_posts_per_day` posts of each day (10 by default) in their home feed; later posts appear only in [List user posts](#list-user-posts).

### JSON body

| Field | Type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| content? | ?string | The plain text of the post (1-2,000 characters) |
| attachments? | array[[pre-uploaded attachment](/http-api/messages/#pre-uploaded-attachment-object) object] | Images uploaded through [Upload post media](#upload-post-media) (at most 4) |
| public? | boolean | Ignored; the server sets whether the post is [public](#public-posts) |
| poll? | [poll input](#poll-input-object) object | A poll; needs `content` as its question and no `attachments` |

### Response

| Status | Body | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 200 | [post object](#post-object) | The post was published |
| 400<sup>1</sup> | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | The post is empty, an attachment was refused, or the poll is invalid |
| 403<sup>3</sup> | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | The text, a poll option or an image is blocked and the request returns `CONTENT_BLOCKED` |
| 429<sup>2</sup> | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | The caller already published `max_feed_posts_per_day` posts this feed day |

<sup>1</sup> An empty post, or a poll without text, returns `CANNOT_SEND_EMPTY_MESSAGE`. An image returns `INVALID_FORM_BODY` with the field code `ATTACHMENT_MUST_BE_IMAGE` for another sniffed type and `UPLOADED_ATTACHMENT_NOT_FOUND` for an unknown or used upload key, `MEDIA_METADATA_ERROR` when it cannot be read, `FILE_SIZE_TOO_LARGE` over the size limit, and `EXPLICIT_CONTENT_CANNOT_BE_SENT` for explicit media. An MP4 or WebM filename returns `ATTACHMENT_MUST_BE_IMAGE` too while the caller's `feature_feed_video` limit is not `0`. A poll with attachments returns the field code `INVALID_FORMAT` on `poll`, and repeated options the same code on `poll.options`

<sup>2</sup> Returned as `RATE_LIMITED`, with `retry_after` counting down to the start of the next feed day

<sup>3</sup> An MP4 or WebM filename while the caller's `feature_feed_video` limit is `0` returns `FEATURE_TEMPORARILY_DISABLED`

### Rate limit

5 requests per 10 seconds for each authenticated user, on the `feed:post:create` bucket.

## Start video post

<RouteHeader method="POST" path="/v1/feed/videos" />

Starts a [video post](#video-posts) and returns 200 with a [video upload ticket object](#video-upload-ticket-object). The text is checked like message content now; the post is published with it once the video passes processing.

The video takes one of the caller's `max_feed_posts_per_day` posts at once, and a cancelled or refused video does not give it back. An account has at most 3 videos uploading, processing or being checked at a time.

### JSON body

| Field | Type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| filename | string | The name of the file (1-255 characters) |
| file_size<sup>1</sup> | integer | The size of the file in bytes (1-524,288,000) |
| content_type | string | `video/mp4`, `video/webm`, `video/quicktime` or `video/x-matroska` |
| content? | ?string | The plain text of the post (1-2,000 characters) |
| public? | boolean | Ignored; the server sets whether the post is [public](#public-posts) |

<sup>1</sup> Bunny's stored copy is measured again once it arrives, and a video over 500 MB is then deleted

### Response

| Status | Body | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 200 | [video upload ticket](#video-upload-ticket-object) object | The video was started |
| 400 | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | The body is invalid and the request returns `INVALID_FORM_BODY` |
| 403<sup>1</sup> | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | Video posts are off for the caller and the request returns `FEATURE_TEMPORARILY_DISABLED` |
| 429<sup>2</sup> | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | 3 videos are on their way, or the caller already has `max_feed_posts_per_day` posts this feed day |
| 502 | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | Bunny Stream did not create the video and the request returns `BAD_GATEWAY` |

<sup>1</sup> Off means a `feature_feed_video` limit of `0` or an instance without Bunny Stream settings. A blocked text returns `CONTENT_BLOCKED`

<sup>2</sup> Returned as `RATE_LIMITED`; `retry_after` is 60 seconds for the videos on their way, and counts down to the start of the next feed day for the daily limit

### Rate limit

3 requests per minute for each authenticated user, on the `feed:video:create` bucket.

## Receive Bunny Stream webhook

<RouteHeader method="POST" path="/v1/feed/videos/webhook" unauthenticated />

Accepts a Bunny Stream video status change and returns 200 with a webhook received object. The instance's Bunny Stream library sends it; clients never call it. The signature headers are the credential, and the status is read again from Bunny before anything changes.

### Limitations

- A body over 4 KiB returns 400 `INVALID_REQUEST`, and so does a signed body that is not the expected JSON.
- A signature that is missing, of another version or algorithm, or not made over the raw body with the library's read-only API key returns 401 `UNAUTHORIZED`.
- An event of another library or with an unknown status is acknowledged and ignored.
- One status of one video is handled once a minute; a repeat within the minute is acknowledged and ignored.

:::caution[The signature covers the exact bytes]
A proxy that re-encodes, reorders or pretty-prints the body invalidates the signature.
:::

### Request headers

| Field | Type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| X-BunnyStream-Signature | string | Lowercase hex HMAC-SHA256 of the raw body, keyed with the library's read-only API key |
| X-BunnyStream-Signature-Version | string | The signature version, which must be `v1` |
| X-BunnyStream-Signature-Algorithm | string | The signature algorithm, which must be `hmac-sha256` |

### JSON body

| Field | Type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VideoLibraryId | integer | The Bunny Stream library of the video |
| VideoGuid | string | The Bunny Stream ID (GUID) of the video |
| Status | integer | The Bunny Stream video status |

### Response

| Status | Body | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 200 | webhook received object | The event was authenticated and queued, or acknowledged and ignored |
| 400 | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | The body is too large or malformed and the request returns `INVALID_REQUEST` |
| 401 | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | The signature did not verify and the request returns `UNAUTHORIZED` |
| 503 | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | The instance has no Bunny Stream settings and the request returns `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE` |

### Rate limit

## Get home feed

<RouteHeader method="GET" path="/v1/feed/home" />

Returns one page of the posts of the accounts the caller follows and of the caller, as an array of [post objects](#post-object) with the newest post first. The home feed covers the last 30 days.

One request reads a bounded part of the home feed, so a page can hold fewer posts than `limit`, or none, while older posts remain.

The pagination signal is in the response headers. `X-Next-Before` is the `before` value for the next page, and it is absent when no older posts are known to remain. Read the next page with `X-Next-Before` as `before` when it is present, and otherwise with the last post's `id`. An empty page without `X-Next-Before` is the end of the home feed. A client that follows empty pages on its own should wait between the requests and stop after a few in a row, since each one counts against the rate limit below.

Posts of an account the caller stops following, or blocks, or is blocked by, leave the home feed.

An account the caller follows shows all its posts while the caller sees its full profile under [Get user profile](/http-api/users/#get-user-profile). One the caller sees only as a public profile (for example one followed through a public Discover post with [Follow user](/http-api/users/follows/#follow-user) and `post_id`, or from the [feed suggestions](#get-feed-suggestions)) shows only its [public posts](#public-posts) here, from the whole 30-day window, and only while its [profile privacy level](/http-api/users/#profile-privacy-levels) reads as `EVERYONE`; one whose profile is limited or refused shows none. Those public posts are left out while the author is flagged as a spammer or for suspicious activity, and while the caller's `feature_feed_discover` limit is `0`. As on Discover, a post hidden after reports, or one with images or a video while the instance's `feature_feed_discover_media` or `feature_feed_discover_video` limit is `0`, is left out too, and so is every post between accounts that block each other. A narrower privacy level of the author reaches the home feed on its next page, and a wider one within a minute.

### Query parameters

| Field | Type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| before? | snowflake | The cursor selecting posts published before this post ID |
| limit? | integer | The maximum number of posts to return (1-50, default 25) |

### Response

| Status | Body | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 200 | array of [post objects](#post-object) | The page was returned, possibly empty; `X-Next-Before` says where the next page starts |

### Rate limit

20 requests per 10 seconds for each authenticated user, on the `feed:home` bucket.

## Get Discover feed

<RouteHeader method="GET" path="/v1/feed/discover" />

Returns one page of Discover, the [public posts](#public-posts) of the whole instance that drew the most reactions lately, as an array of [post objects](#post-object) with the best post first. While the caller's `feature_feed_discover` limit is `0`, the route returns 403 `FEATURE_TEMPORARILY_DISABLED`.

Discover ranks the public posts of the last 72 hours by their likes, comments and poll votes from established accounts, a newer reaction weighing more than an older one, and spreads out the posts of one author. An author qualifies with a verified email address, an account at least 24 hours old (7 days for a post with a video) and a [profile privacy level](/http-api/users/#profile-privacy-levels) that reads as `EVERYONE`, and not while flagged as a spammer or for suspicious activity. While the instance's `feature_feed_discover_media` limit is `0`, posts with images are left out, and Twitch and Kick previews lose their images. While its `feature_feed_discover_video` limit is `0`, [video posts](#video-posts) are left out. Discover holds no signed video URL; each page signs the videos it returns for the caller. A post that draws enough reports leaves Discover but stays readable through its ID. Posts of accounts that block the caller, or that the caller blocks, are left out.

The list holds up to 1,000 posts and is rebuilt about every 30 seconds, so the counts on a page can be about a minute old; `liked`, `author_following` and the poll state are the caller's own at the time of the request.

The pagination signal is in the response headers. `X-Next-Cursor` is the `cursor` value for the next page, and it is absent at the end of the list. A page read after a rebuild continues at the same position in the new list, so a post can repeat or be skipped; drop the posts already shown.

### Query parameters

| Field | Type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| cursor? | string | The `X-Next-Cursor` value of the previous page |
| limit? | integer | The maximum number of posts to return (1-50, default 20) |

### Response

| Status | Body | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 200 | array of [post objects](#post-object) | The page was returned, possibly empty; `X-Next-Cursor` says where the next page starts |
| 403 | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | The caller's `feature_feed_discover` limit is `0` and the request returns `FEATURE_TEMPORARILY_DISABLED` |

### Rate limit

20 requests per 10 seconds for each authenticated user, on the `feed:discover` bucket.

## Get feed suggestions

<RouteHeader method="GET" path="/v1/feed/suggestions" />

Returns a [feed suggestions object](#feed-suggestions-object) with up to five popular accounts for the caller to follow and up to five popular [Discovery](/http-api/discovery/) communities for the caller to join. While the caller's `feature_feed_suggestions` limit is `0`, the route returns 403 `FEATURE_TEMPORARILY_DISABLED`.

The instance keeps a list of up to 100 accounts and a list of up to 100 communities, and rebuilds both about once a minute. The account list takes the accounts with the most followers, each with at least as many as the `feed_suggestions_min_followers` limit of the default rule (at least 1), that qualify: not a bot or system account; a verified email address, a claimed account at least 24 hours old and the date of birth of an adult; not banned, deleted or flagged as a spammer or for suspicious activity; a `feature_feed` limit other than `0`; a [profile privacy level](/http-api/users/#profile-privacy-levels) that reads as `EVERYONE`; and at least half of its 100 newest followers with a verified email address and a claimed, unflagged account at least 72 hours old. The community list takes the approved Discovery listings with the most members whose [NSFW level](/http-api/guilds/#nsfw-levels) is `0` and that lack the `INVITES_DISABLED` [guild feature](/http-api/guilds/#guild-features). It is empty while the instance has Discovery disabled or no search backend.

Each request draws up to 20 entries of each list at random and returns the first five that suit the caller, so two requests seldom return the same entries; keep one answer while the view stays open. `users` leaves out the caller, the accounts the caller follows and the accounts that block the caller or that the caller blocks. It stays empty unless the caller has a verified email address, a claimed account at least 24 hours old and no spammer or suspicious activity flag, and is an adult by date of birth; an account without a date of birth receives no accounts. `guilds` leaves out the communities the caller is in and does not depend on the caller's standing.

A new ban, spammer or suspicious activity flag, or a deletion removes an account from the list at once. Any other change to a listed account or community reaches the lists within about a minute and a half. When the lists have not been rebuilt for 10 minutes, both arrays are empty. A listed account can be followed through [Follow user](/http-api/users/follows/#follow-user) without `post_id`, and a listed community joined through [Join discovery guild](/http-api/discovery/#join-discovery-guild).

### Response

| Status | Body | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 200 | [feed suggestions](#feed-suggestions-object) object | The suggestions were returned, possibly with empty arrays |
| 403 | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | The caller's `feature_feed_suggestions` limit is `0` and the request returns `FEATURE_TEMPORARILY_DISABLED` |

### Rate limit

10 requests per 10 seconds for each authenticated user, on the `feed:suggestions` bucket.

| --- | --- | --- |
| 204 | empty | The reply was deleted |
| 403 | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | The caller wrote neither the reply nor the post and the request returns `MISSING_PERMISSIONS` |
| 404<sup>1</sup> | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | No post has the ID and the request returns `UNKNOWN_FEED_POST` |

<sup>1</sup> A comment with no reply of the ID returns `UNKNOWN_FEED_COMMENT`

### Rate limit

10 requests per 10 seconds for each authenticated user, on the `feed:comment:delete` bucket, shared with [Delete comment](#delete-comment).

## List notifications

<RouteHeader method="GET" path="/v1/feed/notifications" />

Returns one page of the likes, comments, replies and follows aimed at the caller, as an array of [notification objects](#notification-object) with the newest `id` first. Notifications are kept for 90 days. Liking or commenting on your own post, or replying to your own comment, raises none.

A page can hold fewer notifications than `limit` while more remain. Read the next page with the last notification's `id` as `before`, and stop at an empty page. Notifications of a deleted post are left out.

New and updated notifications also arrive as [FEED_NOTIFICATION_CREATE](/gateway/events/#feed-notification-create) on the caller's sessions, at most one a minute; an update held back by that limit follows when the minute ends.

### Query parameters

| Field | Type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| before? | snowflake | The cursor selecting notifications with an ID below this notification ID |
| limit? | integer | The maximum number of notifications to return (1-50, default 25) |

### Response

| Status | Body | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 200 | array of [notification objects](#notification-object) | The page was returned, possibly empty |

### Rate limit

20 requests per 10 seconds for each authenticated user, on the `feed:notifications` bucket.

## Mark notifications read

<RouteHeader method="POST" path="/v1/feed/notifications/ack" />

Moves the caller's notification read cursor to `read_at` and marks the notifications counted up to `unread_seq` read. The cursor only moves forward and never past the server's time. A notification counted after the sequence the session sent stays unread, so a push that crossed the request is not lost. All of the caller's sessions, this one included, receive [FEED_NOTIFICATION_ACK](/gateway/events/#feed-notification-ack) with the response body.

### JSON body

| Field | Type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| read_at<sup>1</sup> | ISO8601 timestamp | When the caller saw their notifications |
| unread_seq | integer | The newest unread sequence the session has seen (READY, pushes and acks), 0 or more |
| unread_epoch | integer | The epoch of that sequence, 0 or more |

<sup>1</sup> A server time: the newest `updated_at` shown or the time the list was served, never the device clock

### Response body

| Field | Type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| last_read_at | ISO8601 timestamp | The read cursor after the request |
| unread_seq<sup>1</sup> | integer | The unread sequence the read covers |
| unread_epoch | integer | The epoch of the server's current sequence |

<sup>1</sup> The greater of the sequence an earlier read covered and the lower of `unread_seq` and the server's current sequence; `unread_seq` counts as 0 when `unread_epoch` is not the current epoch

### Response

| Status | Body | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 200 | response body | The read was taken; a cursor that would move back stays where it was |
| 400 | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | A field is missing, `read_at` is not ISO8601 or a number is negative; returns `INVALID_FORM_BODY` |
| 403 | [error response](/http-api/#error-response) | The caller's `feature_feed` limit is `0` and the request returns `FEATURE_TEMPORARILY_DISABLED` |

### Rate limit

10 requests per 10 seconds for each authenticated user, on the `feed:notifications:ack` bucket.

## Account deletion

Deleting a post deletes its likes, poll votes, comments and replies, and its video from Bunny Stream. Deleting an account deletes its posts and their media, its videos that are not posts yet, its likes, poll votes, comments and replies, and its notifications, and the posts it liked, voted in or commented on stop counting them. Its [data harvest](/http-api/users/data-harvest/) lists in `feed.json` its posts, each with its text, timestamps, whether it is public, its poll and its media URLs, its comments and replies with their post and text (a reply with the `parent_id` of its comment), the posts it likes, its poll votes, and its notifications of the last 90 days with the IDs of their accounts. A video is listed by the URL of its HLS playlist, signed for the 7 days the archive can be downloaded, or `null` while the instance has no Bunny Stream settings.
